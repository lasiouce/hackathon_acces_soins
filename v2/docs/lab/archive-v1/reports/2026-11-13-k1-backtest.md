# K1 backtest: stock-flow vs persistence and trend

> **Headline:**
> - **Nationally, H1 wins clearly.** Mean |error| is **2.5 %**, vs 4.6 %
>   for the linear trend and 7.8 % for persistence.
> - **Per département, H1 only ties the trend.** MAPE is **8.6 %** vs
>   **8.7 %**, at 5–8 year horizons, over 11 profession × horizon cells
>   (measured).

## Question
K1, from the derivation. Fit on past years, project from an origin year,
compare to the real counts. Kill rule: H1 must beat H0 on département MAPE,
or it cannot claim accuracy.

## How
- **Data:** `data/processed/stock.parquet`. For médecins, "Médecine
  générale" is excluded. Kinés use RPPS from 2017 only. Salaried nurses
  come from the raked BTS file.
- **Method:** `sante/model.py`, `sante/backtest.py`
  (`uv run python -m sante.backtest`, ~6 s).
  - Annual cohort-component model on 5-year bands: each year a fifth of each
    band moves up one band.
  - One *net* retention rate per band (exits + net entries at that age),
    estimated by least squares on the fit window. The youngest band is held
    at its last level.
  - "Local" = rates per département, shrunk toward the national rate with a
    weight of 200 persons. "Trend shares" = H1's national total × each
    département's share, with the share extrapolated linearly.
- **Windows:** fit 2012–2018, origin 2018 (kinés: fit 2017–2021, origin
  2021). Targets: 2023 (h = 5) and 2026 (h = 8).
- **Outputs:** `artifacts/s01_backtest/results.csv` and
  `errors_by_dep.parquet` (actual vs predicted per département, for the
  uncertainty bands).

## ⚑ What could make it wrong
- **H1's département edge over the trend is 0.1 point. That is noise, not
  a win.** It also comes from choosing the best of 3 H1 variants after
  seeing the results, which is selection bias. "Local rates" is now fixed
  in advance as the H1 default for the next test.
- **Private-practice nurses: H1 overshoots nationally by +12.5 % at 5
  years.** Growth stalled after 2020 (98.6k in 2021 → 98.9k in 2023, from
  the stock files), and the 2012–2018 rates can't know it. The cause is
  **assumed**: COVID or nurse installation zoning. Not investigated.
- **One window per profession.** A second origin (e.g. 2016 → 2021) would
  show whether the ranking is stable. Not run.
- Shrinkage weight 200 and the youngest band held flat are **assumed**, not
  tuned.
- Salaried nurses' age × sex is itself an estimate from raking, so their
  backtest partly tests the raking.

## → What it changes
- **H1 survives, but in a narrower form.**
  - *Nationally*: accuracy claim OK, about 2–3 % at 5–8 years.
  - *Per département*: **no accuracy claim beyond "on par with the trend"**.
  - It stays the scenario engine because it is the only candidate with ages
    and levers. The trend has neither.
  - Recorded in `decisions.md`, under H1.
- **The derivation's prediction holds.** Département error is dominated by
  *where* people settle (π), not by ageing: national errors are small,
  local ones are not. This is the case for un-parking A2 (a behavioural
  model of installation), or at least the trend-shares hybrid, which wins
  for specialists at 8 years.
- **Uncertainty bands for the UI:** use the empirical département errors.
  They are about ±5–9 % at 5 years and ±7–14 % at 8 years, by profession,
  from `errors_by_dep.parquet`. That beats an invented band.

## Correction 2026-11-13: entries were double-counted (bug, found by the UI session)

**What was wrong.**
- In the 30–39 bands, where real entries land (the "<30" band holds only
  0.4 % of the stock), the fitted net retention was **1.26–1.31**.
- Applied to the stock every year, this made entries *proportional to the
  stock*, so they compounded.
- Private-practice specialists projected **+3.5 %/yr** after 2026, against
  +1.5 %/yr observed in 2021–26 (measured by the UI session).

**Fix** (`sante/model.py`): retention is capped at 1. The excess becomes an
**absolute yearly inflow**: the cell's mean net inflow over the fit window.

**Re-run of the bench, measured.** All rows: mean over the 11
profession × horizon cells.

| Estimator | Département MAPE, before → after | Mean \|national error\|, before → after |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 local rates** | 8.57 → **8.08** | 2.62 → 4.13 |
| H1 national rates | 9.07 → 8.66 | 2.48 → 5.14 |
| H1 national × trend shares | 8.85 → 9.19 | 2.48 → 5.14 |
| H0 linear trend | 8.68 (unchanged) | 4.63 |
| H0 persistence | 9.33 (unchanged) | 7.80 |
| *H1 local, trending entries* (new, **not registered**) | 17.79 | 4.09 |

Long run: all specialists 2026 → 2035 now grow **+1.6 %/yr** (flat entries)
vs +2.1 %/yr observed in 2021–26. No more compounding.

**What changes in the conclusions:**
- **Per département, H1 local rates is now the best estimator**: 8.08 % vs
  8.68 % for the trend, a 0.6-point gap. That is more than the 0.1 point
  before, though still small.
- **Nationally, the earlier "2.5 % vs 4.6 %" claim is withdrawn.** It came
  partly from the compounding bug, which happened to mimic entries rising
  in 2012–2026. Corrected, H1 under-predicts specialists by 10–11 % and
  dentists by 10–12 % at 8 years. Flat entries miss the rise in entries.
- **The fix the data points to is entries from the known pipeline**:
  enrolled students and quotas, already in `data/raw` for M/O/P.
  Extrapolating each département's own entry trend is not the fix: it
  amplifies noise (17.8 % MAPE).
